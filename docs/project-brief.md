## Problem and objectives

Academic communication is scattered across general social media. Students struggle to find relevant communities and answers, miss announcements, and repeat questions because earlier discussions are difficult to retrieve.

The project will organize academic interaction, help students discover relevant content, retain reusable discussions across academic years, support direct communication, and offer assistance grounded in accessible sources. Iterative design, functional testing, usability feedback, access-control evaluation, and citation evaluation will provide evidence for the graduation report.

## Specified stack and settled decisions

- Next.js and TypeScript for frontend and backend, PostgreSQL storage, and Prisma data access are specified by the abstract. React and the Next.js App Router are the preferred approach in AGENTS.md. Strict TypeScript and server-side authorization apply.
- Both students and teachers can create communities. The four post types, direct text messaging, in-app notifications, archive, and grounded AI assistant are baseline scope; they are not optional extensions.
- The latest notes settle the direction that everyone participates equally in the shared social space, while teachers have additional authority within their own course communities. Teacher verification and the exact community powers still need definition; this does not grant global authority or automatic priority across the platform.
- University email ownership must be verified. The notes' suggestion that a typed student ID or email proves student status conflicts with the identity rules: neither a typed ID nor a matching domain establishes ownership, current enrollment, or teacher status.
- AI uses authorized content, cites supporting sources, and indicates insufficient information. Teacher identity and popularity alone do not establish truth. Private messages are excluded from retrieval by default; external AI use of private community content needs an explicit privacy decision.
- Complete and evaluate the minimum baseline before implementing later extensions. No service provider, hosting choice, or vector infrastructure has been selected. M1a runtime and application/tool versions are recorded in README and the lockfile.
- M1a uses `/en` and `/ar`, with `/` temporarily redirecting to English. This initial default is reversible; browser-language negotiation is deferred. No separate app name is selected.

## M1b local foundation — proposed implementation plan

Status: planned only; implementation is not authorized by this planning request. Goal: the smallest local PostgreSQL/Prisma foundation capable of persisting users, profiles and email-ownership challenge state. M1b does not establish real email ownership, authentication, enrollment or teacher status. No provider, public endpoint, mobile framework, hosting setup or unrelated feature is included.

### Minimum proposed schema

| Model | Fields and database guarantees |
| --- | --- |
| `User` | UUID primary key; original email and normalized `emailKey` (proposed maximum 254 characters each), with a unique constraint on `emailKey`; nullable `emailVerifiedAt`; `createdAt`/`updatedAt`. New records are unverified. Email is immutable in M1b; email-change/reverification rules are deferred. |
| `Profile` | `userId` as both primary key and foreign key to `User`, guaranteeing at most one profile per user; required, nonblank `displayName`, maximum 60 characters, supporting Arabic and English; creation/update timestamps. A user may exist before profile setup, but a completed profile must have a name. The name is self-reported, not verified identity. No major, student ID, biography, public visibility or teacher flag yet. |
| `EmailOwnershipChallenge` | UUID primary key; required user foreign key; snapshot of the target `emailKey`; unique SHA-256 token digest; creation/expiry timestamps and nullable consumption/revocation timestamps; index on user/expiry. Store only the digest of a cryptographically random token, never the raw token. Enforce expiry after creation with a reviewed database check. |

Owner decisions recorded on 2026-10-08:

- Email normalization is approved: trim surrounding whitespace, lowercase the domain, and preserve the part before `@`, including capitalization, dots and plus suffixes. Do not remove internal characters or apply provider-specific rewriting.
- Known examples supplied by the owner are `s12xxxxxx@stu.najah.edu` for students and `first.last@najah.edu` for teachers. Other formats are unconfirmed. These are examples, not naming-pattern validation rules or a finalized exhaustive domain allowlist. Neither domain nor email naming pattern grants teacher permissions; ownership and teacher verification remain separate.
- Display name is required during profile setup, with a maximum of 60 characters and Arabic/English support. Validate nonblank Unicode text with consistent server/database length handling; do not use an ASCII-only name restriction. This is a self-reported display name, not proof of identity.

Capitalization consequences to explain before implementation: if the preserved `emailKey` is used for exact duplicate-account and login matching, `Example@NAJAH.EDU` and `Example@najah.edu` match, but `Example@najah.edu` and `example@najah.edu` do not. Local-part case variants could therefore create separate accounts, and entering different capitalization before `@` could fail login matching.
