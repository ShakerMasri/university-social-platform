## Project’s Abstract

University Social Learning Platform
University students often depend on groups scattered across general social media platforms for academic communication. Relevant communities and useful answers can be difficult to find, unrelated posts distract from educational discussions, and important announcements may be overlooked. Questions are also repeated each academic year as earlier discussions become difficult to retrieve. We propose a dedicated university social platform that organizes these interactions, preserves reusable knowledge, and improves access to relevant information.
The core system will provide university email verification, user profiles, and public or private communities that both students and teachers can create. Users will share questions, discussions, resources, and announcements, with comments and voting on content. The platform will help users find content relevant to their studies. In-app notifications will highlight announcements and replies, while text messaging will support direct communication.
A searchable archive will retain past discussions across academic years, helping students find and reuse existing knowledge. An AI assistant will retrieve information from permitted platform content and selected trusted university sources, cite its sources, and indicate when sufficient information is unavailable. Retrieval will respect community access permissions.
We will develop the platform iteratively using Next.js and TypeScript for the frontend and backend, PostgreSQL for data storage, and Prisma for database access. Development will include requirements analysis, interface and database design, implementation, and testing. Functional and usability testing will evaluate core features, access control, and whether AI responses are supported by their cited sources. User feedback will guide improvements.
Related applications include Moodle, which provides course management and discussion forums, and Piazza, which supports academic questions and answers. Our project will combine open community creation, persistent academic discussions, and source-based assistance in one university social environment.
